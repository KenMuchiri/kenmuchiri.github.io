What Studying Philosophy Taught Me About Building AI

March 2026

People sometimes ask why an AI founder is finishing an Economics and Philosophy degree instead of another engineering credential. The honest answer is that philosophy is where I learned to ask the question underneath the question — and that habit has been more useful to AlphaAI than any framework I’ve learned.

Most technical conversations start with “can we build this.” Philosophy trained me to start earlier: what is this system actually optimizing for, who bears the cost if it’s wrong, and is the problem we’re solving the real problem or just the one that’s easiest to measure.

Economics gave me the other half — a way to think about incentives, markets, and why a technically excellent product can still fail if it doesn’t change anyone’s incentives. Together, they’re the filter every AlphaAI product idea has to pass through before a single model gets trained.

I don’t think this makes me a better engineer than someone who only studied computer science. I think it means I spend less time building the wrong thing well.
