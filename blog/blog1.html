AI & Africa

Why Africa Needs to Build AI, Not Just Buy It

January 2026

Every AI headline this year has been about the same handful of labs, in the same handful of countries. Africa shows up in these stories almost exclusively as a market — a place AI gets sold into, once it’s already been built somewhere else.

That framing has a cost. Models trained on foreign data don’t generalize well to African languages, African transaction patterns, or African business behavior. Tools priced for economies with far higher willingness to pay stay out of reach for the businesses that could actually use them. And the assumptions baked into most AI products — reliable connectivity, formal banking, English as a default — simply don’t hold for most of the continent.

None of this is a reason to wait for someone else to fix it. It’s the reason I started AlphaAI. If Africa is only ever a consumer of AI built elsewhere, we pay rent on intelligence instead of owning any of it. I don’t think that has to be permanent — I think it’s a starting condition, and starting conditions can change.

That’s the whole thesis, really. Build AI on African data, for African problems, to a genuinely world-class standard. Everything else is execution.
